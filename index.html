What a DPF and an EGR valve do

Both sit in the exhaust path of a diesel. Both clog with soot and carbon over time. Here is what cleaning them looks like.

  • Diesel cars
  • SUVs
  • Utes
  • 4WDs
  • Light commercial

Most modern diesel cars, utes, 4WDs and vans have an EGR valve, and many also have a DPF. Not sure about yours? Send us the year, make and model.

Cleaning clears a blockage. If a unit is cracked or has failed, it needs replacing, and we'll tell you before you pay for any more work.

Before you replace anything

Clean it first. Replace only if you have to.

A new DPF is a big-ticket part, often thousands of dollars. If the unit is only blocked, cleaning is the sensible first step.

FAQ

Questions about DPF and EGR cleaning

How do I know if my DPF or EGR valve is blocked?

Common signs are a DPF warning light, limp mode, a rough idle, hesitation when you accelerate and black smoke. Other faults can look the same, so the car needs checking to be sure.

Can you remove (delete) my DPF or EGR valve instead?

No. Removing emissions equipment from a road-registered vehicle is illegal in NSW, so we don't do deletes. We clean the unit so your vehicle stays compliant.

Will cleaning fix the problem?

Cleaning fixes a blockage. It won't fix a cracked filter or a fault somewhere else, such as a sensor. If that's the case, we'll tell you what's needed before you spend more.
